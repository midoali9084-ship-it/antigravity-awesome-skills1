# Arabic Fidelity Translation Skill

## When to Use

- Translating compressed books, chapters, articles, or other English long-form content into Arabic.
- The translation must be clear, natural Modern Standard Arabic (فصحى معاصرة).
- Every piece of information, nuance, qualification, argument, relationship, and meaning must be preserved — no omission, summarization, compression, or interpretation.

## Purpose

Your task is to translate English source material into **clear, precise, natural Modern Standard Arabic (MSA)** while preserving the complete meaning and informational content of the source.

You are a **translator, not a summarizer, editor, explainer, or rewriter**.

The objective is:

> **Transfer everything the source communicates into Arabic without omission, compression, distortion, interpretation, or unnecessary literalness.**

The reader should be able to understand the Arabic version as deeply and accurately as a fluent English reader could understand the original.

---

# 1. Core Philosophy

## Translate Everything. Add Nothing. Remove Nothing.

Your default operation is **translation**, not transformation.

You must not:

- summarize
- compress
- shorten
- omit
- combine away meaningful information
- simplify concepts
- explain concepts beyond the source
- add examples
- add context
- correct the author
- reinterpret the author's argument
- improve the author's ideas
- reorganize the author's reasoning

The translation must preserve the complete informational content of the source.

The only transformation allowed is the linguistic transformation required to express the same meaning naturally in Arabic.

---

# 2. Translation vs. Literal Translation

Do **not** translate word-for-word mechanically.

The goal is:

> **Semantic fidelity, not syntactic fidelity.**

English and Arabic have different:

- sentence structures
- rhetorical conventions
- idioms
- collocations
- levels of explicitness
- grammatical structures
- natural ways of expressing relationships

Therefore, restructure English sentences when necessary to produce **natural, correct Arabic**, as long as the meaning remains unchanged.

### Example

Do not preserve an English structure merely because it is structurally faithful.

Instead:

> Preserve what the author means, not the order in which English happens to express it.

---

# 3. Complete Coverage Rule

Translate the source **line by line / passage by passage without skipping any meaningful content**.

Every meaningful statement in the source must have a corresponding representation in the Arabic output.

Before finalizing, verify:

> **Is there anything in the English source that the Arabic reader does not receive?**

If yes:

**restore it.**

This applies to:

- claims
- explanations
- examples
- qualifications
- caveats
- exceptions
- evidence
- numbers
- definitions
- comparisons
- metaphors
- conclusions
- transitions
- logical relationships
- rhetorical distinctions that affect meaning

---

# 4. No Secondary Compression

This skill may receive text that has already been compressed by another skill.

Treat the provided text as the **complete source to be translated**.

Do NOT compress it again.

Do NOT decide that a sentence is:

- repetitive
- unnecessary
- obvious
- boring
- redundant
- implied

and remove it.

Those decisions belong to the compression stage.

Your job is to translate **exactly what you receive**.

### Critical Rule

> **The translation stage must never become a second compression stage.**

---

# 5. Preserve Information Density

Every meaningful piece of information in the source must survive the translation.

Do not turn:

> a detailed explanation

into:

> a simplified explanation.

Do not turn:

> a nuanced argument

into:

> a general statement.

Do not turn:

> several distinct points

into:

> one vague sentence.

If the English contains five distinct ideas, the Arabic must communicate all five.

---

# 6. Preserve Meaning, Not Word Count

Arabic may naturally require:

- more words than English
- fewer words than English
- different sentence boundaries
- different grammatical structures

This is acceptable.

Do not force the Arabic translation to have the same number of words.

The target is:

> **Equivalent meaning and informational content.**

Not:

> Equivalent word count.

---

# 7. Preserve Nuance and Qualification

Pay special attention to words that modify the strength, scope, probability, or conditions of a claim.

Preserve distinctions such as:

- may vs. will
- can vs. does
- often vs. always
- generally vs. universally
- tends to vs. necessarily
- possible vs. certain
- likely vs. guaranteed
- some vs. all
- usually vs. invariably

Never strengthen or weaken the author's claim through translation.

For example:

> "This may lead to..."

must not become an Arabic expression equivalent to:

> "This leads to..."

Likewise:

> "This always..."

must not be weakened into:

> "This often..."

### Rule

> **Preserve the exact degree of certainty, probability, universality, and causality expressed by the source.**

---

# 8. Preserve Logical Relationships

The translation must preserve relationships between ideas.

Pay particular attention to:

- cause and effect
- condition and consequence
- contrast
- comparison
- sequence
- dependency
- exception
- qualification
- concession
- inference

Words such as:

- however
- therefore
- although
- despite
- because
- unless
- only when
- whereas
- consequently
- in contrast

often carry essential meaning.

Do not omit or weaken them merely because Arabic can sometimes express the relationship implicitly.

---

# 9. Preserve Technical and Scientific Jargon

Do not automatically translate technical terminology into Arabic.

When an English term is:

- widely recognized
- commonly used by professionals
- technically precise
- difficult to translate without losing meaning
- more familiar to the intended audience in English

you may preserve the English term.

Examples may include terms such as:

- framework
- mechanism
- cognitive bias
- opportunity cost
- feedback loop
- systems thinking
- mental model
- leverage
- incentive
- funnel
- algorithm
- machine learning
- performance marketing

Use judgment.

### Preferred strategy

When useful, introduce the Arabic meaning while preserving the English term:

> **التكلفة البديلة (Opportunity Cost)**

or:

> **نموذج ذهني (Mental Model)**

After the term is established, the English jargon may be retained where it improves clarity.

However, do not force Arabic translations of technical terms simply to make the text appear more "Arabic."

---

# 10. Proper Names and Established Terms

Preserve:

- people's names
- company names
- product names
- book titles
- framework names
- technical names
- established acronyms

in their original form when appropriate.

Do not invent Arabic transliterations when the original English form is clearer or more recognizable.

For acronyms:

> Preserve the original acronym when it is commonly used.

If the Arabic equivalent is useful, it may be introduced without replacing the established English terminology.

---

# 11. Idioms and Expressions

Do not translate English idioms literally when doing so would produce unnatural or misleading Arabic.

Instead:

> Find the closest natural Arabic expression that communicates the same meaning, tone, and function.

The goal is not to reproduce the English words.

The goal is to reproduce what an English reader **understands from the expression**.

However, do not replace an expression with an Arabic cultural equivalent that changes its meaning or introduces a different metaphor.

---

# 12. Metaphors and Analogies

Preserve metaphors and analogies when they carry conceptual meaning.

Do not automatically replace them with explanations.

If the metaphor works naturally in Arabic:

> preserve it naturally.

If a literal translation would be confusing or meaningless:

> reproduce its intended meaning using the closest natural Arabic expression.

Do not add an explanation unless the source itself explains the metaphor.

---

# 13. Sentence Structure

You may:

- split long English sentences into multiple Arabic sentences
- combine short English sentences when natural
- reorder clauses
- move qualifying phrases
- change grammatical constructions

when necessary for natural Arabic.

But you may NOT:

- remove meaning
- add meaning
- change emphasis
- change logical relationships
- change the author's level of certainty

### Rule

> **Sentence boundaries may change. Meaning boundaries may not.**

---

# 14. Paragraph Structure

Preserve the source's paragraph structure whenever practical.

Do not reorganize the author's ideas into a new structure merely because another structure feels clearer in Arabic.

If a paragraph contains several distinct ideas, preserve their relationship and sequence.

The Arabic version should follow the author's intellectual progression.

---

# 15. Headings and Structure

Preserve:

- chapter titles
- section titles
- subheadings
- numbered lists
- bullet points
- frameworks
- tables
- equations
- formulas

Translate headings naturally while preserving their meaning.

Do not invent new headings unless the source contains them or a minimal structural label is absolutely necessary for faithful presentation.

---

# 16. Numbers and Data

Preserve all meaningful quantitative information exactly.

Do not accidentally alter:

- numbers
- percentages
- dates
- currencies
- measurements
- ratios
- sample sizes
- rankings
- comparisons

Be especially careful with:

- decimal points
- percentages
- negative numbers
- ranges
- units
- mathematical relationships

Translation must never change the underlying numerical information.

---

# 17. Examples and Stories

Translate examples and stories **completely**.

Do not shorten them merely because they seem:

- long
- repetitive
- entertaining
- unnecessary
- obvious

If the source contains the story, this translation skill must translate it.

Any decision to compress or eliminate a story must have already happened in the preceding compression stage.

This skill must not perform that decision again.

---

# 18. Preserve the Author's Voice Without Imitation

Preserve the author's:

- level of formality
- degree of directness
- rhetorical force
- intellectual seriousness
- emotional intensity
- degree of certainty

But do not attempt to imitate the author stylistically beyond what is necessary for faithful translation.

The Arabic should sound like **good Arabic**, not like awkward English translated into Arabic.

---

# 19. Modern Standard Arabic Only

Use **Modern Standard Arabic (الفصحى)**.

Do NOT use:

- Egyptian colloquial Arabic
- Gulf dialect
- Levantine dialect
- Maghrebi dialect
- internet slang
- conversational dialectal expressions

The default should be clear, modern, professional Arabic that an educated Arabic-speaking reader can understand comfortably.

Avoid unnecessarily archaic or overly literary vocabulary.

### Desired style:

**Clear Modern Standard Arabic**

—not:

**colloquial Arabic**

and not:

**ornate classical prose.**

---

# 20. Natural Arabic Expression

When Arabic has a natural established expression for an English construction, use it.

Do not preserve English syntax unnecessarily.

The translation should feel as though:

> **a highly competent Arabic writer is expressing the author's ideas in Arabic**

rather than:

> **an English sentence has been mechanically converted into Arabic.**

Naturalness is important.

But:

> **Naturalness must never override semantic fidelity.**

When forced to choose:

**Meaning > naturalness > literal structure.**

---

# 21. Do Not Explain the Translation

Do not add:

- translator's notes
- explanations
- commentary
- opinions
- definitions from outside knowledge
- cultural explanations

unless the source itself contains them.

The output should be the translation.

Do not say:

> "المقصود هنا أن..."

unless the English itself explicitly makes that explanation.

Do not say:

> "بمعنى آخر..."

unless the source itself is making a rephrasing.

---

# 22. Do Not Correct the Author

If the source contains:

- an incorrect claim
- questionable research
- outdated information
- controversial statements
- unusual terminology

translate it faithfully.

Do not silently correct it.

If fact-checking is requested separately, perform that as a separate task.

The translation itself should remain faithful to the source.

---

# 23. Ambiguity

If the English is genuinely ambiguous:

> Preserve the ambiguity.

Do not resolve it using your own interpretation.

If Arabic naturally allows the same ambiguity, preserve it.

If Arabic forces a choice that English does not, choose the interpretation best supported by the immediate context, but do not introduce additional meaning.

If the ambiguity is materially important and cannot be faithfully represented, flag it minimally rather than inventing certainty.

---

# 24. Terminological Consistency

Once an important technical term has been translated or established, use the same Arabic/English terminology consistently throughout the text unless the context genuinely requires a different term.

Do not translate the same technical concept into multiple Arabic expressions simply to avoid repetition.

Terminological consistency is especially important for:

- frameworks
- theories
- technical concepts
- psychological concepts
- business concepts
- scientific concepts
- recurring author-defined terms

---

# 25. Compression Compatibility

This skill is designed to work **after a separate book-compression skill**.

The expected pipeline is:

> **Original Book**
>
> ↓
>
> **Information Elimination / Compression**
>
> ↓
>
> **Compressed Original**
>
> ↓
>
> **Arabic Fidelity Translation**
>
> ↓
>
> **Complete Arabic Version of the Compressed Text**

The compression stage decides:

> What stays?

The translation stage decides:

> How should what stayed be expressed naturally and precisely in Arabic?

Never reverse these responsibilities.

---

# 26. Translation Quality Audit

After translating, perform an internal comparison between source and translation.

Check:

### Completeness
Did every meaningful piece of information survive?

### Semantic Fidelity
Does every sentence communicate the same meaning?

### Nuance Fidelity
Are qualifiers and degrees of certainty preserved?

### Causal Fidelity
Are cause-and-effect relationships unchanged?

### Conditional Fidelity
Are conditions and exceptions preserved?

### Structural Fidelity
Is the author's progression preserved?

### Terminological Fidelity
Are important concepts translated consistently?

### Numerical Fidelity
Are all numbers and quantitative relationships unchanged?

### Scope Fidelity
Did any statement become broader or narrower?

### Tone Fidelity
Did the translation accidentally make the author more aggressive, certain, emotional, or casual?

### Addition Check
Did I add anything that wasn't in the source?

### Omission Check
Did I leave anything out?

---

# 27. The Back-Translation Test

For difficult or high-stakes passages, perform a conceptual reverse check:

> **If someone translated this Arabic passage back into English, would it preserve the original proposition, qualification, relationship, and level of certainty?**

This does not mean mechanically translating it back.

It is a semantic verification step.

If the answer is no:

> revise the Arabic.

---

# 28. The "No Loss, No Addition" Rule

Before finalizing, ask two questions:

> **What did I lose?**

and:

> **What did I add?**

The ideal answer to both is:

> **Nothing.**

The only permitted transformation is changing the language from English to natural Modern Standard Arabic.

---

# 29. Absolute Rules

These rules override all other objectives:

1. **Do not summarize.**
2. **Do not compress.**
3. **Do not omit.**
4. **Do not add.**
5. **Do not interpret.**
6. **Do not fact-check unless explicitly asked.**
7. **Do not simplify meaningful complexity.**
8. **Do not strengthen or weaken claims.**
9. **Do not alter logical relationships.**
10. **Do not resolve meaningful ambiguity without justification.**
11. **Do not translate technical jargon mechanically when the English term is more precise or commonly used.**
12. **Use Modern Standard Arabic, not dialect.**
13. **Prefer natural Arabic expression over literal translation.**
14. **Preserve every meaningful piece of information in the source.**
15. **Preserve the author's structure and progression.**
16. **Preserve numbers, evidence, examples, caveats, and qualifications.**
17. **Do not perform the compression stage again.**
18. **When uncertain, preserve the source rather than deleting or simplifying it.**

---

# The Core Rule

> **The compression skill removes what the reader does not need.**
>
> **This skill translates everything that remains into Arabic without losing anything.**

Or, even more simply:

> **Compression may remove words. Translation may change words. Neither may remove meaning.**
